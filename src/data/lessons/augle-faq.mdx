# 19. FAQ

## 18.1 How is Augle different from a chatbot?

A chatbot gives you one model's best guess in fluent prose. Augle runs seven specialized agents across five different providers through a structured three-round deliberation, with explicit confidence grading, source verification, and an integrity layer that monitors the process in real time. The output is a structured document with confidence-graded claims, contested terrain, knowledge gaps, and unresolved objections — not a paragraph of prose.

## 18.2 Why do you need seven agents? Wouldn't one really good model be simpler?

Simpler — and worse. A single model has a single set of biases, a single training distribution, and a single class of failure modes. Stacking the same model against itself does not produce real adversarial pressure. Augle's seven agents have explicit role separation, run on five different provider models, and operate under confidence flow rules that prevent any one agent from inflating confidence beyond what the evidence supports. The structural diversity is the point.

## 18.3 Why do different agents run on different providers?

Each provider has distinct capabilities, biases, and blindspots. Locking the ensemble to one provider would inherit that provider's failure modes across every reasoning role. By spanning Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, DeepSeek, and xAI, we distribute reasoning risk and concentrate complementary strengths — Claude's instruction-following on the Topic Architect and Guardian, Gemini's research synthesis on the Cartographer, GPT-4o's methodological reasoning on the Methodologist, DeepSeek's deterministic synthesis on the Synthesizer, Grok's real-time data grounding on the Pragmatist.

## 18.4 Why is the Contrarian on Opus 4.6 for Deep but Sonnet for everything else?

The Contrarian runs at the highest temperature in the ensemble (1.0) and performs the most creatively demanding reasoning task — finding the strongest version of the opposing argument, steelmanning, and producing calibrated objections with resolution conditions. In Deep sessions specifically, the Contrarian runs three full rounds plus async expert interjection extensions. The quality ceiling of adversarial reasoning is the one place in the architecture where individual model capability translates directly to user-perceptible output improvement. Opus on Deep raises that ceiling. Sonnet handles Rapid and Standard at sustainable cost. The cost delta of approximately $0.02 to $0.04 per Deep session is absorbed into the existing 6-credit Deep tier — no price change.

## 18.5 What happens if a cited source does not exist?

The Source Verification Service catches it. Every cited URL, DOI, and document reference is verified against live network resources before downstream agents process it. URLs that 404, DOIs that fail to resolve, and academic citations that hit retraction databases are flagged. The affected evidence node is auto-downgraded — Contested for not-found, Probable cap for unverified — and the failure is logged in the Session Log with the exact reason. The node is not removed; the failure is documented.

## 18.6 What does "deterministic verdict" actually mean?

It means the same question with the same evidence base will always produce the same confidence verdict and the same core finding direction. The Synthesizer runs at temperature zero. The verdict is computed deterministically from the structured evidence node registry by a server-side function before the Synthesizer is dispatched. The Synthesizer cannot override the verdict — it can only write the finding that explains it. The deliberation can vary across runs (different objection angles, different evidence framings). The verdict cannot. This is a hard product integrity requirement, not a preference.

## 18.7 Can I see how the agents reasoned, or just the conclusion?

Yes. Every session has a Conversation view (the full discourse between agents) and a Signals view (a 3x2 grid showing each agent's status, latest output, and structured contributions). The final Results screen surfaces the structured output across six tabs: Supporting Claims, Contested Terrain, Knowledge Gaps, Unresolved Objections, Application Notes, and Session Log. The Session Log captures every Guardian flag, every model interaction, and every integrity decision with timestamps. The PDF export includes all of this.

## 18.8 What is the Guardian, and why is it visible?

The Guardian is the integrity layer. It evaluates user-contributed context at every phase boundary in Standard and Deep sessions — checking for scope, epistemic type, and safety, then transforming accepted contributions into neutral language before routing them into the deliberation. It is visible because transparency about how Augle protects research integrity is a feature, not a warning. Users see the Guardian's reasoning in real time. They see what is accepted, what is rejected, and what is transformed.

## 18.9 Is Augle financial advice?

No. Augle is AI-generated analytical content for research and informational purposes. It does not constitute financial, investment, legal, or other professional advice. The platform's safety architecture explicitly enforces this — the Synthesizer and Pragmatist have behavioral rules around financial advice framing, and every Results page and PDF export carries a legal disclaimer to this effect. Always conduct your own due diligence before making any decision based on Augle output.

## 18.13 How do I know Augle's accuracy claims are real?

Markets mode resolution. Every prediction market contract resolves YES or NO. Every Augle session that researched it produced a verdict with a confidence grade. The match between verdict and resolution is the calibration signal — and we are publishing the calibration dashboard publicly by day 90 of Phase 1, so the accuracy claims are not assertions, they are checkable. Letters and Science verdicts do not enter the calibration record (research questions do not resolve in the same way), but the underlying deliberation is auditable in the same structured format.

We do not sell raw session data tied to individual users. Aggregate calibration data, structural reasoning patterns, and methodologically anonymized artifacts are part of the long-term asset. Privacy and corpus terms will be detailed in the Terms of Service at full enterprise rollout.
