# Screenshots

A walkthrough of Augle from first touch to final report — every screen, in sequence.

<SectionHeading url="https://app.augle.com/" newTab>Inquiry – First Touch</SectionHeading>

Augle doesn't ask you to learn the product before you can use it. It asks you one question: what do you want to know? Just the Augle logo, a tagline, and a single search bar that says exactly what it does: Find a Polymarket or Kalshi contract. The Letters & Science offers the same simplicity with the same clear directive, but without the API guardrails — what do you want to deliberate?

The restraint is deliberate. Like Google's homepage, the minimal surface communicates something before the user has typed a single character: this product knows what it's for. There is no clutter because there is no ambiguity. You are here to ask a question. Everything else is secondary to that. The interface doesn't compete with the inquiry — it creates the conditions for it.

This is a deliberate design choice that reflects how the product thinks about its users. Prediction market participants already have a question in mind when they arrive. Augle meets them there — not at a signup wall, not at a pricing page. At the question.

<SectionHeading url="https://app.augle.com/auth" newTab>User Login</SectionHeading>

Augle's authentication is frictionless by design. Google SSO or email and password — one tap or two fields. The login modal surfaces over the home screen rather than redirecting away from it, because the question the user came in with stays visible the entire time. They never lose their thread. This is a small detail that reflects a bigger product philosophy: the user's inquiry is the center of the experience, and everything else — including account management — is peripheral to it.

<SectionHeading url="https://app.augle.com/auth" newTab>Create Account</SectionHeading>

A new user is one screen away from their first session. Display name, email, password — or bypass it entirely with Google. The account isn't just an access credential. <SectionHeading url="https://app.augle.com/auth/otp" newTab>User Authorization</SectionHeading>

Two-factor verification is a single, clean step. A 6-digit code, a countdown, and one input field. No friction theater, no multiple screens. <SectionHeading url="https://app.augle.com/session/setup" newTab>Session Setup</SectionHeading>

The question is confirmed. The market is live. Now the user makes one decision: how deep do they want to go.

Augle surfaces three session tiers side by side — Rapid, Standard, and Deep — each with a transparent breakdown of what fires, how long it runs, and exactly what it costs. Rapid is one credit, one phase, two agents, three minutes. Standard is three credits, three phases, the full six-agent ensemble, and the Methodologist acting as an evidence gatekeeper throughout. Deep adds an asynchronous domain expert and runs up to 45 minutes for questions where the evidence landscape genuinely warrants it.

The question card at the top shows the live Polymarket probability — 34% YES as of July 4, 2026 — alongside a green confirmation from the Topic Architect: question confirmed, no ambiguity detected, evidence threshold set to Standard, no domain flags on intake. The system has already run a neutrality check before the user ever clicks Start.

Credits are reserved, not spent. If the session returns a Low confidence or Insufficient evidence finding, the refund policy applies automatically. The user sees their current balance, what will be reserved, and what will remain — before they commit anything.

This screen is where Augle makes its pricing philosophy explicit: you pay for depth, you only pay for value delivered, and you know exactly what you're getting before the session starts. One button — Start session — and the ensemble goes to work.

<SectionHeading url="https://app.augle.com/session/running" newTab>Session Conversation</SectionHeading>

This is Augle working. The session is live, the agents are in sequence, and the deliberation is unfolding in real time in the conversation feed. The Cartographer has mapped the evidence landscape — macro indicators, labor signals, policy environment — and the Methodologist has locked the research parameters. What you're watching is not a single model generating a response. It's a structured ensemble, each agent with a distinct role and a distinct AI model, working through a question in a way that mirrors how rigorous human research teams operate. The Guardian watches the entire session from the sticky bar above the feed. The phase tabs below show exactly where the session is in its deliberation arc. Nothing is hidden and nothing is summarized prematurely. The user sees the work.

<SectionHeading url="https://app.augle.com/session/running" newTab>Session Signals</SectionHeading>

The Signals view is for users who want to see inside the machine while it runs. Every agent gets a card. The Cartographer is streaming its evidence clusters in real time — Fed minutes, GDP data, labor market signals, yield spread. The Methodologist, Contrarian, Synthesizer, Pragmatist, and Ledger are waiting their turns. Below the agent grid, the evidence nodes panel is already classifying claims as they emerge: Established, Probable, Contested, or Gap. This isn't a loading screen with a spinner. The user can watch the epistemic structure of the finding being built claim by claim, in real time, with the model powering each agent visible on every card. Augle is transparent about what it knows, what it doesn't, and exactly which AI made which call.

<SectionHeading url="https://www.app.augle.com/session/running/guardian" newTab>Guardian Flag</SectionHeading>

Midway through Exploration, the Guardian catches something. The session soft-pauses. A Moderate flag card surfaces inline in the conversation feed — attached directly to the Methodologist's message that triggered it — and the Guardian bar at the top now reads "1 Flag." The user sees exactly where the issue was caught, in context, with two clear choices: Interject with additional information, or Ignore and let the session continue. This is the integrity layer working as designed. No other AI research tool on the market catches evidence asymmetry mid-session, pauses the deliberation, and asks the user what they know. Augle does. The finding doesn't proceed until the flag is resolved. That's not a limitation — it's the product.

<SectionHeading url="https://www.app.augle.com/session/running/guardian/details" newTab>Guardian Flag Details</SectionHeading>

The user opens the flag. The Guardian speaks in plain language: the evidence assembled so far draws exclusively from recessionary indicators, and no countervailing evidence has been incorporated. The Methodologist's flagged message is shown in full, in its own contained card. Below it, a text field invites the user to contribute — a source, a data point, direct domain knowledge. The hint text is precise: providing a source strengthens approval with the Guardian. The user isn't just a passive recipient of the session. They're a participant in the integrity of the finding. When they submit, the Guardian evaluates the input, transforms it into a structured packet, and routes it to the appropriate agents before the session resumes. This is augmented deliberation — the human and the ensemble working together.

<SectionHeading url="https://app.augle.com/session/running/guardian/deliberation" newTab>Phase Boundary</SectionHeading>

The Cartographer mapped 8 evidence nodes. The Methodologist locked the confidence ceiling at Probable. The Contrarian raised one strong objection that has already been accepted into the scope. Three agents are queued for Deliberation. And before that phase launches, the user has one optional window: contribute domain knowledge directly to the Guardian, who will evaluate it and route it appropriately. This is the pause between movements. It's where the user can lean in or stand back. Either way, they know exactly what the session has built and exactly where it's going.

<SectionHeading url="https://www.app.augle.com/session/running" newTab>Building Report</SectionHeading>

The deliberation is done. Six agents across three phases have mapped the evidence, challenged the claims, stress-tested the objections, and assembled a synthesis. Now Augle is compiling the output — structuring the finding, grading the evidence nodes, logging the unresolved objections, and preparing the application notes. <SectionHeading url="https://www.app.augle.com/session/report" newTab>Session Report</SectionHeading>

This is the deliverable. At the top: the probability direction, the confidence grade, and a plain-language core finding the user can act on immediately. Below it, every layer of the analysis is accessible — supporting claims graded by evidence quality, contested terrain where the signal was genuinely ambiguous, knowledge gaps the evidence base couldn't fill, unresolved objections from the Contrarian that traveled all the way to delivery, and application notes for turning the finding into a decision. Session metadata runs across the bottom: platform, runtime, agents deployed, credits used, evidence sourced timestamp. Every claim is traceable to a source. Every grade is defensible. The report is exportable as a PDF. It's not a summary. It's a structured research document — one that tells you not just what Augle thinks, but exactly how confident it is and why.

<SectionHeading url="https://app.augle.com/chat" newTab>Session History</SectionHeading>

Every session a user runs becomes part of a permanent, searchable record — accessible from the sidebar at any time, even while a session is actively running.

The sidebar divides cleanly into two zones. At the top, Active sessions surface immediately — the user can see every in-progress deliberation at a glance and jump back into any of them without losing context. Below that, Session history gives them a full archive of every completed session they've ever run, with search and filtering built directly in. Platform, category, depth, confidence grade — the user can slice their history any way that's useful to them.

This matters more than it might appear. Augle isn't a one-question tool. The users who get the most out of it are running sessions regularly — stress-testing positions, tracking evolving questions across multiple sessions as new evidence emerges, building a body of structured research over time. The session history is where that body of work lives. It's not a log. It's a research library.

The search bar at the top of the history panel means a user who ran a session on Bitcoin six weeks ago can find it in two keystrokes. Every session is titled by the research question, so the history is self-organizing — there's no folder structure to maintain, no tags to apply. The question is the label.

Combined with the calibration record that builds in the background as contracts resolve, the session history transforms Augle from a single-use research tool into something more durable: a documented, searchable record of how you've thought about consequential questions over time — and how right you were.
